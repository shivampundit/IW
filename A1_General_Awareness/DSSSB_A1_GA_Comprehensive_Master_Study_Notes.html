MANDATORY FOR DSSSB Article 239AA: Constitutional Architecture of National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi

1. Historical Insertion: 69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991 entered into force on 1 February 1992, creating Article 239AA and designating the Union Territory as the 'National Capital Territory of Delhi'. It created an elected Legislative Assembly (70 seats) and a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.

2. Legislative Powers & The 3 Excluded State Subjects: Under Article 239AA(3)(a), Delhi Legislative Assembly can legislate on all matters in the State List (List II) and Concurrent List (List III) of the Seventh Schedule, EXCEPT THREE ENTRIES in the State List:

• Entry 1: Public Order
• Entry 2: Police
• Entry 18: Land

Ye teen vishay strictly Central Government (Union Home Ministry) aur Lieutenant Governor (LG) ke direct control me aate hain. Delhi Police seedhe MHA ke under kaam karti hai.

3. Council of Ministers Size Restriction (10% Rule): Unlike regular Indian states where the 91st Amendment allows up to 15% of assembly strength as ministers, Article 239AA(4) mandates that Delhi Council of Ministers cannot exceed 10% of total Assembly seats:

Total Seats = 70 => 10% of 70 = Exactly 7 Ministers (1 Chief Minister + 6 Cabinet Ministers maximum!)

4. Lieutenant Governor (LG) Role: The LG is appointed by the President under Article 239. Chief Minister is appointed by the President of India (not by the LG!). Other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the CM. Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. In case of difference of opinion between LG and ministers, LG refers the matter to the President under Art. 239AA(4) proviso.

3.1 Reserve Bank of India (RBI): Quantitative & Qualitative Monetary Tools

RBI established on 1 April 1935 under RBI Act 1934 based on Hilton Young Commission (Royal Commission) recommendations. Nationalized on 1 January 1949. First Governor: Sir Osborne Smith; First Indian Governor: C.D. Deshmukh. Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) has 6 members (3 RBI + 3 GoI) headed by RBI Governor, targeting CPI inflation at 4% (±2% tolerance band: 2% to 6%) under Section 45ZB of RBI Act.

Monetary Tool Full Technical Definition Impact on Economy & Market Liquidity
Repo Rate (Policy Rate) The fixed interest rate at which RBI lends short-term liquidity to commercial banks against collateral of government and approved securities. When inflation is high, RBI increases Repo Rate. Borrowing becomes expensive, banks raise lending rates, credit demand drops, and money supply contracts.
Reverse Repo Rate The interest rate at which RBI absorbs excess liquidity from commercial banks on an overnight basis against eligible government securities. Increasing Reverse Repo encourages banks to park idle cash safely with RBI instead of lending in the market, reducing overall liquidity.
Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) Introduced in 2022 as the new floor of the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) corridor, allowing RBI to absorb overnight liquidity WITHOUT providing collateral securities. Positioned 25 bps below Repo Rate. Replaced fixed reverse repo for liquidity management.
Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Overnight borrowing window for scheduled commercial banks in acute emergencies by dipping up to 2% into their statutory SLR quota. Positioned 25 bps above Repo Rate (acts as the ceiling of the LAF corridor). Higher penal rate.
Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) The specified percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) that commercial banks must maintain strictly in CASH with the RBI under Section 42(1) of RBI Act. Banks earn ZERO INTEREST on CRR balances. Increasing CRR drains cash reserves directly from the banking system.
Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) The percentage of NDTL that commercial banks must maintain within themselves in safe liquid assets: cash, gold, or approved unencumbered Government Securities (G-Secs/T-Bills) under Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act 1949. Banks earn interest on G-Secs. SLR secures depositor funds and ensures steady institutional demand for government borrowing.
Open Market Operations (OMO) Outright sale or purchase of government securities by the RBI in the open secondary market. Sale of G-Secs: Drains rupee liquidity from market. Purchase of G-Secs: Injects fresh liquidity into financial markets.

3.2 Money Supply Aggregates (M1–M4) & National Income Accounting

RBI Money Supply Measures
• M0 (Reserve Money / High-Powered Money / Base Money): Currency in Circulation + Bankers' Deposits with RBI + Other Deposits with RBI.
• M1 (Narrow Money): Currency with Public + Demand Deposits with Banking System (CASA) + Other Deposits with RBI. (Most liquid!).
• M2: M1 + Post Office Savings Bank Deposits.
• M3 (Broad Money): M1 + Time/Term Deposits with the Banking System. (Most widely used aggregate in monetary policy!).
• M4: M3 + Total Post Office Deposits (excluding National Savings Certificates NSC).
National Income Accounting Relationships
• GDP (Gross Domestic Product): Value of all final goods & services produced within the geographical boundaries of India in 1 financial year.
• GNP = GDP + NFIA: (Net Factor Income from Abroad = Income earned by Indian residents abroad - Income earned by foreigners in India).
• Net Domestic Product (NDP): GDP - Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital).
• National Income (NI): Exactly equals Net National Product at Factor Cost (NNP_FC):
NNP_FC = GNP - Depreciation - Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes - Subsidies)

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

Light must travel from Denser to Rarer medium with angle of incidence greater than Critical Angle ($i > heta_c$).

Applications: Optical Fiber communication, Sparkling diamond, Mirage in hot desert, Endoscopy medical tubes.
Acoustics & Echo Conditions

Human ear retains sound sensation for 0.1 seconds (Persistence of Hearing).

Speed of sound v = 344 m/s
Total path 2d = v * t = 344 * 0.1 = 34.4 m

Eye Defects & Corrective Lenses
  • • Myopia (Short-sightedness): Image forms in front of retina. Corrected using Concave lens (Diverging).

Delhi Local History

The Historic Seven Cities of Delhi (Chronological Order)

1. Qila Rai Pithora (10th-12th C) Founded by Tomar Rajput Anangpal (Lal Kot 1052 CE); expanded by Prithviraj Chauhan. Center of early Delhi Sultanate.
2. Siri (1303 CE) Built by Alauddin Khilji as a fortified city to repel Mongol invasions. Hauz Khas reservoir excavated to supply water.
3. Tughlaqabad (1321 CE) Built by Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq. Massive stone fortress with sloping walls and fortified tomb in artificial lake.
4. Jahanpanah (1326 CE) 'Refuge of the World' built by Muhammad bin Tughlaq to enclose the space between Qila Rai Pithora and Siri Fort.
5. Ferozabad (1354 CE) Built by Firoz Shah Tughlaq on Yamuna banks (Feroz Shah Kotla). Transported Topra and Meerut Ashokan Pillars here.
6. Dinpanah / Shergarh (1534-40) Started by Humayun, captured and rebuilt by Sher Shah Suri as Purana Qila (Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque & Sher Mandal).
7. Shahjahanabad (1638 CE) Built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan when shifting capital from Agra. Walled city with 14 gates, Red Fort (Lal Qila), and Jama Masjid.
8. New Delhi (1911-31) Designed by British architects Sir Edwin Lutyens and Sir Herbert Baker (Rashtrapati Bhavan, Parliament House, India Gate).
