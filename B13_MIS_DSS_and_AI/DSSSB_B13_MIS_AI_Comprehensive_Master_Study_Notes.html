Information Systems Hierarchy: TPS, MIS, DSS & ESS

Anthony's decision hierarchy, structured vs unstructured decisions, and periodic vs exception reports.

Organizational Level Classification

• Operational Level: Transaction Processing Systems (TPS: barcode checkout, ATM withdrawals). High volume, highly structured, repetitive daily operations.
• Tactical / Middle Management: Management Information Systems (MIS). Summarizes TPS data into periodic scheduled and exception reports (e.g., inventory below reorder level).
• Strategic / Executive Level: Decision Support Systems (DSS) and Executive Support Systems (ESS). Non-routine, semi-structured and unstructured strategic planning (3-5 yr horizon).

Decision Framework (Herbert Simon)

• Structured Decisions: Repetitive, algorithmic (e.g., payroll calculation, inventory reorder formula). Suitable for full automation.
• Semi-Structured: Combines standard analytical models with managerial judgment (e.g., budgeting, plant location).
• Unstructured: Complex, novel, subjective with no predefined procedure (e.g., entering a foreign market, mergers). Handled by senior executives.

Expert Systems: Architecture, Inference Engines & MYCIN

Knowledge base, Forward vs Backward chaining, Explanation facility, and Expert System Shells.

Expert System Architecture

• Knowledge Base: Stores domain rules (IF-THEN production rules) and facts.
• Inference Engine: Applies deductive logic rules (modus ponens) to infer conclusions.
• Explanation Facility: Explains HOW a conclusion was reached or WHY a question is being asked by displaying fired rules.
• Knowledge Engineer: Interviews human experts and translates tacit heuristics into formal rules.

Forward vs Backward Chaining

• Forward Chaining (Data-Driven / Bottom-Up): Starts with known facts and applies rules to deduce new facts until a goal is reached.
• Backward Chaining (Goal-Driven / Top-Down): Starts with a target hypothesis/goal (e.g., in MYCIN: 'Does patient have meningitis?') and works backwards to verify supporting premise facts.
• Certainty Factors (CF): Values from -1.0 to +1.0 quantifying uncertainty in heuristic rules.
