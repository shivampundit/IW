Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA) — Complete Rule Matrix

Every rule governing singular/plural verb selection in DSSSB TGT/PGT error spotting.

Fundamental Axiom

A singular subject demands a singular verb (V1 + s/es, is, was, has); a plural subject demands a plural verb (V1 base form, are, were, have). In DSSSB examinations, errors are deliberately hidden by placing intervening prepositional phrases or relative clauses between the true subject and its predicate verb.

Connector / Construction Verb Agreement Rule DSSSB High-Yield Exam Example
as well as, along with, together with, with, in addition to, accompanied by, besides, like, unlike Agrees strictly with the FIRST Subject (S1) The teacher, along with her 40 students, was (not were) awarded at Vigyan Bhawan.
Neither... nor, Either... or, Not only... but also, Nor, Or Rule of Proximity: Agrees with the NEAREST Subject (S2) Neither the principal nor the lecturers were (not was) present.
Each of, Either of, Neither of, One of, Everyone of + Plural Noun + strictly SINGULAR Verb Each of the computer terminals is (not are) connected to LAN.
One of + Plural Noun + Relative Pronoun (who / which / that) Relative pronoun verb is PLURAL (agrees with antecedent noun) He is one of the programmers who have (not has) optimized Linux kernel.
THE ONLY ONE OF + Plural Noun + Relative Pronoun 'Only one' forces the verb to remain SINGULAR He is the only one of the candidates who has cleared the cutoff.
A number of vs The number of 'A number of' = PLURAL; 'The number of' = SINGULAR A number of bugs have been reported. The number of bugs is 14.
More than one vs More + Plural Noun + than one 'More than one' + Sing Noun = Sing Verb; 'More [plural] than one' = Plural More than one teacher was absent. More teachers than one were absent.
Collective Nouns (Jury, Committee, Team, Audience, Council) United = Singular Verb ('its'); Divided = Plural Verb ('their') The jury has delivered its verdict. The jury were divided in their opinions.

⚠️ Tricky Quantifiers & Mathematical Fractions

Expressions like half of, two-thirds of, percent of, majority of, a lot of, plenty of take a singular verb if the following noun is uncountable, and a plural verb if the following noun is countable plural:

  • Two-thirds of the book has been revised. (Uncountable/single entity)
  • Two-thirds of the applicants have submitted their credentials. (Countable plural)
  • Ten kilometers is a long walking distance. (Collective unit of measure = Singular)

1. Conditionals Master Reference Matrix

Zero Conditional (Scientific Fact / Universal Truth)
If + Simple Present (V1/s/es) ➔ Simple Present (V1/s/es)

First Conditional (Real / Possible Future)
If + Simple Present (V1) ➔ will / shall / can / may + V1

Example: If you practice 50 PYQs daily, you will clear DSSSB Section A.

Second Conditional (Hypothetical / Unreal Present)
If + Simple Past (Subjunctive 'were' / V2) ➔ would / could + V1

Example: If I were the exam controller, I would eliminate ambiguous questions.

Third Conditional (Unreal Past / Regret)
If + Past Perfect (had + V3) ➔ would / could / might + have + V3

Example: If he had prepared digital logic, he would have scored 18 marks.

2. Negative Inversion Rules (Top DSSSB Question Pattern)

When a sentence begins with a restrictive or negative adverb, the auxiliary verb comes BEFORE the subject (Verb + Subject inversion). Memorize these mandatory pairings:

Hardly / Scarcely / Barely + had + S + V3 ... WHEN + Simple Past

Never use 'than' or 'then'. 'Hardly had he arrived when the bell rang.'

No sooner + had + S + V3 (or did + S + V1) ... THAN + Simple Past

'No sooner had the teacher entered than the students greeted.'

Seldom / Rarely / Little + Auxiliary Verb + S + Main Verb

'Seldom do we witness such algorithmic efficiency.'

Not only ... but also (Parallelism)

Both parts must join matching grammatical forms (noun to noun, verb to verb).

3. The 'It is high time' Subjunctive Trap

Whenever you encounter It is time, It is high time, or It is about time followed by a subject, the following verb MUST be in the Simple Past (V2):

It is high time you started (not start / have started) your computer networks revision.

Note: If followed immediately by an infinitive with no subject, use 'to + V1': 'It is time to start.'

⚠️ Zero Preposition Trap (Never Add Prepositions Here!)

These transitive verbs take direct objects without any preposition in active voice:

  • Comprise: Incorrect: 'comprises of'. Correct: 'The team comprises 5 engineers' (or 'is composed of').

  • Order: Incorrect: 'order for a coffee'. Correct: 'order a coffee'.
  • Reach / Enter: Incorrect: 'enter into the room'. Correct: 'enter the room' (except 'enter into an agreement/treaty').
  • Resemble: Incorrect: 'resembles to his father'. Correct: 'resembles his father'.
  • Marry / Attack / Sign: 'He married her' (not 'with her').

Active to Passive Voice Tense Shifts

Rule: Subject becomes Object; Object becomes Subject; Verb is ALWAYS changed to past participle (V3) preceded by appropriate form of 'be'.

  • Simple Present: V1 ➔ is / am / are + V3
  • Present Continuous: is/am/are + V-ing ➔ is/am/are + being + V3
  • Present Perfect: has/have + V3 ➔ has/have + been + V3
  • Simple Past: V2 ➔ was / were + V3
  • Past Continuous: was/were + V-ing ➔ was/were + being + V3
  • Past Perfect: had + V3 ➔ had + been + V3
  • Modals (can, will, must): Modal + V1 ➔ Modal + be + V3
  • Imperatives (Order): Shut the door ➔ Let the door be shut.

Direct to Indirect Speech Tense & Pronoun Shifts

Rule: If reporting verb is in past ('said', 'told'), tense shifts backwards one step into past.

  • Simple Present (V1) ➔ Simple Past (V2)
  • Present Continuous ➔ Past Continuous (was/were + V-ing)
  • Present Perfect / Simple Past (V2) ➔ Past Perfect (had + V3)
  • Will ➔ Would | Can ➔ Could | May ➔ Might | Must ➔ Had to
  • Wh-Questions: Said to me, "Where do you live?" ➔ He asked me where I lived (no 'that'!).
  • Yes/No Questions: Use conjunction if or whether.
  • Universal Truth Exception: "The earth revolves around the sun" ➔ Tense remains Present!

Para-Jumbles (PQRS) & Reading Comprehension

Sentence rearrangement connectors, mandatory pairs, and RC question solving methodology.

Para-Jumble (PQRS) Master Solving Formula

  1. Locate the Opening Sentence: Look for an independent noun introduction (person, concept, technology). Sentences starting with pronouns (he, she, they, it), relative pronouns, or conjunctions (however, therefore, furthermore) almost NEVER open the paragraph.
  2. Identify Mandatory Pairs (MPs):
    • Noun-Pronoun Pair: 'Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam' must precede 'He'.
    • Acronym Pair: Full form 'Local Area Network (LAN)' must precede 'LAN'.
    • Chronological Pair: Birth/Origin ➔ Progression ➔ Modern State.
    • Cause-Effect Connectors: 'Because', 'Since', 'Consequently', 'Thus'.
  3. Option Elimination: Test the first sentence against the 4 multiple choice options. Usually 2 options are eliminated instantly.

Reading Comprehension: Decoding Question Types

  • Central Idea / Title: Must encompass the ENTIRE passage, not just a single compelling paragraph. Reject narrow options or overly broad cosmic generalizations.
  • Tone of the Passage: Objective/Analytical (neutral presentation of data), Critical/Cautionary (pointing flaws or dangers), Eulogistic (praising warmly), Cynical/Satirical (mocking or skeptical).
  • Inference vs Direct Fact: Direct facts are explicitly stated. An inference is a conclusion that MUST be true based on the provided facts, even though the exact wording is absent.
