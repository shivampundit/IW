English Language — 30 Golden Rules & Trap Matrix Cheat Sheet

High-yield subject-verb agreement formulas, negative inversion pairs, fixed prepositions, and rapid error spotters.

SVA 1: 'As well as' Rule

S1 + as well as / along with + S2: Verb agrees strictly with S1. (The captain, along with players, is ready).

SVA 2: Proximity Rule

Neither... nor / Either... or: Verb agrees with the closer subject. (Neither he nor his friends were invited).

SVA 3: 'Each of' Structure

Each of / Either of / One of + Plural Noun: Strictly takes a SINGULAR verb. (Each of the files has been saved).

Inversion 1: Hardly / Scarcely

Hardly had + S + V3 ... WHEN (Never 'than' or 'then'!). 'Hardly had I stepped out when it rained.'

Inversion 2: No Sooner

No sooner had + S + V3 ... THAN (Never 'when'!). 'No sooner had he seen the police than he fled.'

Inversion 3: Subjunctive

It is high time + Subject + Simple Past (V2). 'It is high time we went home.'

Prep 1: Latin Adjectives

Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Prior, Prefer: Strictly take TO, NEVER 'than'.

Prep 2: Comprise Trap

Comprise = Consist of. In active voice, 'comprise of' is FALSE. Write 'comprises 5 units'.

Prep 3: Abstain / Refrain

Abstain / Refrain / Prevent / Prohibit + FROM + V-ing. 'Refrain from smoking.'

20 Golden Exam One-Liners (Quick Revision)

1. 'Lest' is followed strictly by should without negative words ('Lest he should fail').
2. 'A number of' takes a plural verb; 'The number of' takes a singular verb.
3. 'Unless' expresses condition; 'Until' expresses time. Neither takes 'not'.
4. Collective nouns take plural verb when divided in opinion ('jury were divided').
5. Comparative degree with two entities requires 'the' + comparative ('He is the taller of the two').
6. In third conditional: 'If + had + V3' pairs strictly with 'would have + V3'.
7. 'Call off' means cancel; 'Put off' means postpone; 'Put out' means extinguish fire.
8. Never use double comparatives or double superlatives ('more better' is WRONG).
9. 'Accused of', 'Acquitted of', 'Guilty of', 'Deprived of' all take OF.
10. Universal truths in indirect speech retain present tense ('The sun rises in east').
11. 'Cope with' is correct; 'Cope up with' is a standard grammatical blunder.
12. 'Meticulous' means painstaking/thorough; 'Ephemeral' means short-lived.
13. 'Ubiquitous' means omnipresent; its direct antonym is 'rare' or 'scarce'.
14. 'Occurrence' is spelled with double-c and double-r: O-C-C-U-R-R-E-N-C-E.
15. 'Bureaucracy' is spelled B-U-R-E-A-U-C-R-A-C-Y.
16. 'Misanthrope' hates mankind; 'Misogynist' hates women; 'Philanthropist' loves mankind.
17. 'A blessing in disguise' means an apparent misfortune that yields good results.
18. Imperative passive uses 'Let + Object + be + V3' ('Let the door be shut').
19. In indirect wh-questions, the word order is assertive (Subject + Verb), never interrogative.
20. 'Since' denotes specific point in time; 'For' denotes duration/period of time.

🚀 Top 10 High-Frequency Exam One-Liners & Solved Shortcuts (Chapter 07)
  • Golden One-Liner #1: Worked Example: If sentence R introduces 'Dr. Vikram Sarabhai established INCOSPAR in 1962' and sentence P says 'He later spearheaded the formation of ISRO in 1969', then 'RP' forms a locked chronological & noun-pronoun pair. — [Exam Trap: Reading Comprehension me 'Inference / Tone' wale question me kabhi bhi extreme options ('always, never, completely') chunne se bachein — passage ke scope ke andar hi answer chunein!]
  • Golden One-Liner #2: Exam Application (DSSSB TGT 2021): Rearrange the jumbled parts to form a coherent, meaningful sentence: P: and requires continuous updating of skills Q: information technology is an evolving discipline R: that impacts virtually every economic sector S: because new programming frameworks emerge rapidly => Correct Solution: Q - R - P - S. (Logical connectors: Q is the topic sentence introducing the subject ('information technology is an evolving discipline'). R naturally modifies the discipline ('that impacts virtually every economic sector'). P coordinates the impact with professional requirement ('and requires continuous updating of skills'). S provides the causal explanation ('because new programming frameworks emerge rapidly'). Sequence: Q - R - P - S.) — [Hinglish Tip / Memory Hook: Start with subject introduction in Q, then relative clause R. | Common Distractor Traps to Avoid: Q - P - S - R; R - Q - P - S.]
  • Golden One-Liner #3: Exam Application (DSSSB TGT CS 2021): Rearrange the parts into a grammatically logical sentence: P: to build tamper-resistant ledgers Q: distributed consensus algorithms R: blockchain technology utilizes S: across decentralized peer-to-peer networks => Correct Solution: R - Q - P - S. (Subject is in R: 'blockchain technology utilizes'. What does it utilize? Object in Q: 'distributed consensus algorithms'. What is the purpose? Infinitive phrase in P: 'to build tamper-resistant ledgers'. Where? Prepositional phrase in S: 'across decentralized peer-to-peer networks'. Sequence: R - Q - P - S.) — [Hinglish Tip / Memory Hook: Identify subject 'blockchain technology utilizes' in R. | Common Distractor Traps to Avoid: Q - P - R - S; R - S - Q - P.]
  • Golden One-Liner #4: Exam Application (DSSSB PGT 2021): Read the passage snippet and determine the primary tone of the author: 'While artificial intelligence promises unprecedented algorithmic automation in classrooms, we must critically interrogate whether predictive scoring exacerbates socioeconomic stratification among learners who lack high-speed connectivity.' => Correct Solution: Critical and cautionary. (The keywords 'critically interrogate', 'exacerbates socioeconomic stratification', and 'lack high-speed connectivity' indicate that the author is highlighting risks and urging careful questioning rather than blind celebration. The tone is distinctly 'Critical and cautionary'.) — [Hinglish Tip / Memory Hook: The passage warns against inequality caused by AI in education. | Common Distractor Traps to Avoid: Eulogistic and celebratory; Apathetic and indifferent.]
  • Golden One-Liner #5: Exam Application (DSSSB TGT 2021): In reading comprehension passages, what does an 'Inference Question' require an aspirant to do? => Correct Solution: Deduce an unstated conclusion logically supported by the facts presented by the author. (An inference is a logical deduction that is not explicitly stated word-for-word in the text, but is inevitably implied or supported by the author's facts, tone, and premises.
