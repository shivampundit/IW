Chapter 1 • Corporate Communication

Business Communication, 7 Cs & Formal Reporting

Principles of communication, Minutes of Meeting (MoM), Grapevine network, and semantic barriers.

The 7 Cs of Effective Business Communication

1. Clear: Unambiguous purpose; familiar words.
2. Concise: Maximum meaning in minimum words.
3. Concrete: Specific factual data, not vague.
4. Correct: Factually and grammatically accurate.
5. Coherent: Logical sentence and paragraph flow.
6. Complete: Answers all '5 Ws and 1 H' questions.
7. Courteous: Polite, respectful, empathetic tone.
Formal Documentation Formats

• Minutes of Meeting (MoM): Official permanent record of decisions, resolutions voted upon, and action items assigned with deadlines.
• Executive Summary: Standalone 1-2 page executive synthesis at the beginning of a report summarizing key findings and recommendations.
• Letter Notations: 'c.c.' = Carbon Copy (notifies visible copies sent); 'b.c.c.' = Blind Carbon Copy (hidden recipients).

Communication Barriers & Channels

• Grapevine: Informal, unofficial employee communication network operating outside hierarchy; fast transmission of sentiment.
• Semantic Barriers: Linguistic distortions caused by technical jargon, ambiguous words, or poor translation.
• Proposal vs Report: A Report analyzes past facts; a Proposal pitches future solutions for approval.

Henri Fayol's Core Principles & Functions

• 5 Functions (POCCC): Planning, Organizing, Commanding, Coordinating, Controlling.
• Unity of Command: Each employee should receive orders from strictly ONE superior only.
• Unity of Direction: One head and one plan for a group of activities having the same objective.
• Scalar Chain & Gangplank: The formal line of authority from top to bottom. A 'Gangplank' allows direct horizontal communication between peers during emergencies.

POSDCORB Framework (Gulick & Urwick)

Planning ➔ Organizing ➔ Staffing ➔ Directing ➔ COordinating ➔ Reporting ➔ Budgeting.

Organizational Architecture

• Span of Control: Number of subordinates directly reporting to a manager (Wide = Flat hierarchy; Narrow = Tall hierarchy).
• Matrix Structure: Dual reporting lines (Functional manager + Project manager).
• MBO (Peter Drucker): Management by Objectives with joint SMART goal-setting.

The 3 Golden Rules of Double-Entry Accounting

1. Personal Accounts
Debit the Receiver
Credit the Giver
2. Real Accounts (Assets)
Debit what comes in
Credit what goes out
3. Nominal Accounts
Debit all expenses & losses
Credit all incomes & gains
Accounting Principles & Equations
• Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital (Owner's Equity)
• Matching Principle: Expenses incurred to earn revenue must be recognized in the same period as that revenue.
• Trial Balance: Verifies arithmetical accuracy (Total Debits = Total Credits).
Depreciation & Reconciliation

• SLM (Straight Line): Equal annual depreciation calculated on original cost.
• WDV (Written Down Value): Calculated on declining book value.
• BRS: Reconciles timing discrepancies between Cash Book and Bank Passbook.
• Provision vs Reserve: Provision is a charge against profits for known liability; Reserve is retained earnings.
