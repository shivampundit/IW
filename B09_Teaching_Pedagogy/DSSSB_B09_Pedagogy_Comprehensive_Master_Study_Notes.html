Chapter 1 • Cognitive Foundations

Teaching-Learning Paradigms, Revised Bloom & Vygotsky's ZPD

Constructivism (Piaget, Vygotsky), Bloom's revised cognitive domain, scaffolding, and learner-centred pedagogy.

Revised Bloom's Taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001)

Cognitive levels arranged from Lower Order (LOTS) to Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS):

  • 1. Remembering (LOTS): Recalling facts, syntax, definitions.
  • 2. Understanding: Explaining ideas, interpreting error messages.
  • 3. Applying: Using algorithms in new situations (writing code).
  • 4. Analyzing (HOTS): Deconstructing code, tracing bugs.
  • 5. Evaluating: Judging code quality, comparing algorithm efficiencies.
  • 6. Creating (Highest HOTS): Designing new software, architecting systems.
Vygotsky's Social Constructivism

• Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD): The fertile gap between what a learner can do independently and what they can achieve with guidance from a More Knowledgeable Other (MKO).
• Scaffolding (Jerome Bruner): Temporary structural support provided by the teacher (e.g., code templates, step-by-step hints) that is gradually removed as the student gains mastery.
• Teacher's Role: Facilitator, guide on the side, collaborator.

Formative, Summative, Diagnostic & Remedial Teaching

Assessment FOR vs OF learning, CCE scholastic/co-scholastic, rubrics, NRT vs CRT, and feedback loops.

Assessment Typologies

• Formative (Assessment FOR Learning): Ongoing during instruction; low-stakes feedback to guide teaching and learning (quizzes, live coding observations).
• Summative (Assessment OF Learning): Evaluative at the end of an instructional unit; high-stakes grades/certification (board exams, end-term projects).
• Diagnostic Assessment: Identifies specific root misconceptions (e.g. assignment `=` vs comparison `==`).
• Remedial Teaching: Customized instructional interventions designed to eliminate gaps identified by diagnostic tests.

Norm-Referenced vs Criterion-Referenced

• Norm-Referenced (NRT): Measures relative standing against peers (percentiles, ranks, bell curve grading).
• Criterion-Referenced (CRT): Measures performance against predetermined, absolute standards of mastery (e.g., student can write a binary search algorithm in Python scoring 85%+ on rubric regardless of class average).
